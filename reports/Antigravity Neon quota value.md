# Neon Triples What Antigravity Quota Buys

On Google AI Ultra 5x ($99.99/month), Antigravity's shared Gemini pool holds **$600 a week of Gemini 4 Neon at API list prices**. That is about **$2,609 a month, or 26.1× the plan fee**. Argon and Carbon draw the same pool at 3× their launch prices, so they get only $200 a week, $870 a month, or **8.7×**. The figure comes from a per-call measurement on 19 November 2026. Recomputing all 137 rows of that CSV from tokens × list price ÷ weekly quota used gives $600.0/week for every Neon call (range $599.03–$600.56). It also shows that Neon's cached reads are charged at the cheap $0.15/M cached rate ([subvalue.app CSV](https://subvalue.app/data/google-ai-ultra-5x-quota-2026-11-19.csv)). This replaces the earlier assumption in this project, which applied Argon's 8.7× to Neon: Neon is worth three times that. Pro, also measured, comes out at **22.8×** on Neon. Ultra 20x has not been measured; extrapolating from Ultra 5x gives about **52×**. Two caveats limit how far the numbers stretch. Each tier was measured on one account on one day. And in real use, cache eviction and effort level change how much work fits in the pool far more than the quota rate does. The 1 December end of launch pricing doubles Argon and Carbon API prices and leaves Neon's unchanged. Google has not said how quota will respond. Depending on its choice, the Argon/Carbon multiple will either stay at 8.7× with half the work per week, or rise to about 17.4× with the same work.

## Ultra 5x buys $600 of Neon a week, 26.1× the fee

The core evidence is a receipted measurement. One Ultra 5x account ran Antigravity CLI 1.5.12 on 19 November, logging each call's tokens and its change in the weekly `/quota` reading. Every Gemini call landed within 1% of its model's implied pool, and calls above 5¢ landed within 0.1%. Quota did not drift during 27 idle minutes, and no call moved a quota group other than its own ([subvalue.app Ultra 5x](https://subvalue.app/plans/google-ai-ultra-5x/)).

Recomputing the rows, the 19 non-cached Neon calls imply **$600.0/week** and the two cached-read Neon calls **$599.2/week**. Hydrogen and Gemini 3.8 Flash each imply **$600.0**, while Argon and Carbon each imply **$200.0**. Neon is therefore charged at exactly 1× its $3/$15/$0.15 price, and Argon and Carbon at exactly 3× ([subvalue.app CSV](https://subvalue.app/data/google-ai-ultra-5x-quota-2026-11-19.csv)). The same source puts the Ultra 5x five-hour window at $150, which means one week holds four full windows.

An independent check supports this. On 28 October, an Ultra 5x user ran a forum A/B with Flash High (15,111 tokens in, 3,451 out) against Argon High. The Flash call cost $0.0243 at list price and used 0.0001618 of the bucket, which implies a **$150 bucket**. Argon came out at **3.00×** per published dollar. A 13 November follow-up found Hydrogen at 1.00× and Carbon and Orchestrator (Carbon) at 3.00×, with the same multiplier in the five-hour and weekly buckets ([t/196351](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-4-argon-draws-antigravity-quota-at-3x-gemini-3-8-flash-per-published-dollar-clean-a-b-same-bucket/196351)).

The arithmetic is short. $600 × 30.4375/7 = **$2,608.93 a month**, and $2,608.93 ÷ $99.99 = **26.09×** for Neon. $200 × 30.4375/7 = **$869.64**, which is **8.70×** for Argon or Carbon.

The measurement also settles a question that the earlier research had left open. Before this CSV turned up, the only Neon evidence was one 50-minute Ultra 5x session at "Extra" effort. It used "~15% of my weekly Ultra 5x quota" and had ~17.5M uncached input tokens, but no output count was reported ([t/202481](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/bug-report-severe-server-side-implicit-cache-eviction-on-gemini-4-neon-64-5-miss-rate-at-400k-500k-context-95-redundant-uncached-input/202481)). Depending on what one assumed about the pool size, that session supported anything from 1× to 3×. With the pool known to be $600, its 15% equals about $90. The session's estimated list cost is roughly $67–80, so the two agree within the rounding of "~15%". The session therefore supports the 1× rate.

Neon's place in the accounting matches the measurement. A Pro account's quota API lists `gemini-4-neon-*` in the same group and with the same remaining fraction as Flash and Hydrogen, both of which measure at 1× ([OmniRoute #20832](https://github.com/cheaper-inference/OmniRoute/issues/20832)). The 1× rate also matches Google's own wording: since May, Gemini models share "a single rate limit, drawn down according to API pricing" ([Antigravity blog](https://antigravity.google/blog/changes-to-antigravity-plans/)). Neon is not a specially subsidised model. It follows the published rule, and **Argon and Carbon are the exceptions, penalised at 3×**.

The Ultra 5x figure has one weak point. Only one account was measured, and it had upgraded from Pro hours earlier, which reset its limits to full ([subvalue.app Ultra 5x](https://subvalue.app/plans/google-ai-ultra-5x/)). The 28 October calibration was taken separately and agrees with it, which makes a one-off fluke unlikely. Google itself says limits are "primarily determined by available capacity" and are "subject to modification" ([Antigravity plans](https://antigravity.google/docs/plans)).

## Pro holds 22.8× while Ultra 20x remains an extrapolation

Pro was measured the same way on 19 November, over 80 calls. Its Gemini pool (Flash, Hydrogen, Neon) is **$105 a week**, or $30 per five-hour window with 3.5 windows per week. Pro also has a separate **$15-a-week Argon pool**. On Pro, Argon is charged at 1× and has no five-hour limit ([subvalue.app Pro](https://subvalue.app/plans/google-ai-pro/)). Carbon is not offered on Pro at all; it is Ultra- and Enterprise-only ([Antigravity models](https://antigravity.google/docs/models/)).

The Ultra 5x five-hour window is exactly 5× Pro's, which matches Google's "5X worth of tokens" wording. Its week, however, is 5.7× Pro's, because Ultra 5x gets four windows a week and Pro gets 3.5 ([Antigravity blog](https://antigravity.google/blog/changes-to-antigravity-plans/); [subvalue.app Ultra 5x](https://subvalue.app/plans/google-ai-ultra-5x/)).

Nobody has measured Ultra 20x. The common $2,400/week figure is "derived as 4× the measured Ultra 5× pool" from Google's 5×/20× ratio ([subvalue.app plans](https://subvalue.app/plans/)). The one Ultra 20x anecdote shows the pool emptying 62 hours into the weekly cycle, with the Gemini five-hour limit switched off. It gives no token or dollar figures, so it cannot confirm or refute the 4× ratio ([t/203267](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/google-ai-ultra-gemini-models-weekly-quota-drained-to-0-00-in-3-days-of-normal-agy-use-error-tells-an-active-ultra-subscriber-to-upgrade-error-id-f3ce1100-35842/203267)).

| Plan (fee) | Model | Weekly pool, list $ | Monthly | × fee | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ultra 5x ($99.99) | Neon, Hydrogen, Flash | $600 | $2,609 | **26.1×** | Measured 19 Nov |
| Ultra 5x | Argon, Carbon | $200 | $870 | **8.7×** | Measured (3× draw) |
| Pro ($19.99) | Neon, Hydrogen, Flash | $105 | $457 | **22.8×** | Measured 19 Nov |
| Pro | Argon (separate pool) | $15 | $65 | 3.3× | Measured; adds to the Gemini pool |
| Ultra 20x ($199.99) | Neon, Hydrogen, Flash | $2,400 | $10,436 | **52.2×** | Derived, unmeasured |
| Ultra 20x | Argon, Carbon | $800 | $3,479 | 17.4× | Derived, unmeasured |

Pro gets slightly less value per dollar than Ultra 5x on Neon (22.8× against 26.1×). Pro's two pools are separate, though, so a Pro user who fills both — Neon in the Gemini pool and Argon in its own — reaches about $522 a month, also about 26×. Ultra 20x would double the per-fee value only if the 4× ratio holds. Without a measurement, the defensible Ultra 20x claim is "at least Ultra 5x's 26.1×, plausibly about 52×".

A benchmark run hints that Ultra 5x is the more typical account. Bug Hunt Bench ran Neon on an Ultra subscription and exhausted the five-hour window 22–27 minutes into six parallel xhigh legs ([Bug Hunt benchmark.json](https://bughunt.productcompass.pm/data/benchmark.json)). Back-solving the costs puts that window at roughly $45–150 of list value. That matches Ultra 5x's $150 window at 1×, and is hard to square with the ~$600 window that Ultra 20x would need.

## Caching and effort, not the rate, decide how much work fits

A $600 pool says what the quota is worth in API dollars. It does not say how much work that buys. Two variables dominate the second question.

The first is effort level. Artificial Analysis prices Neon at **$0.75 (Low), $1.91 (Medium), $3.74 (High) and $6.44 (XHigh)** per index task, for scores of 44, 55, 60 and 61 ([Artificial Analysis Neon](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-4-neon)). Because Neon draws quota at 1×, those prices are also its quota cost. An Ultra 5x week therefore buys about **805 Low, 315 Medium, 160 High or 93 XHigh tasks**.

Carbon High scores 60.0, essentially the same as Neon High. At its 3× launch-price draw it costs about $11.63 of quota per task, so the same week buys only about **52 tasks**. Argon High costs $14.92 and gets about 40 ([Artificial Analysis Carbon](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-4-carbon); [Artificial Analysis Argon](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-4-argon)). At equal intelligence, Neon fits about **3.1× as many tasks** as Carbon.

Bug Hunt shows the same pattern on long, real-repo hunts. Neon High matched Carbon High's score (57 vs 57.3 bugs fixed) in 445 turns against 817. Its list cost per run was $29.11, which works out to about 20.6 runs per Ultra 5x week against about 6.4 for Carbon at 3× ([Bug Hunt benchmark.json](https://bughunt.productcompass.pm/data/benchmark.json)). The benchmark itself warns that the CLI's token counts may read 10–38% low, and its Carbon High run used an API key rather than the subscription.

This project's comparison now uses 26.1× for Neon. Its recorded tasks per $20 are **696 (Low), 274 (Medium), 140 (High) and 82 (XHigh)**. With these figures Neon takes the top end of the bug-cost Pareto frontier at Medium, High and XHigh, pushing Carbon, Sonnet Max and Fable Max off it.

The second variable is caching, and it can swamp everything else. At 300K context, a Neon cache hit costs $0.045 of quota, while a miss costs $0.90, twenty times as much. In the 50-minute Extra session above, 48 of 150 calls missed the cache and re-sent 16.85M tokens. That was about 95% of the session's uncached input, and the user expected only about 850K without the evictions. Miss rates climbed with context size: **8.6% at 100–200K and 64.5% at 400–500K** ([t/202481](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/bug-report-severe-server-side-implicit-cache-eviction-on-gemini-4-neon-64-5-miss-rate-at-400k-500k-context-95-redundant-uncached-input/202481)). The session used about $90 of quota; with healthy caching the same work would have cost about $20–32. At that miss rate, the $600 week covers only about 5.6 hours of single-agent work, against 16–20 agent-hours at Bug Hunt rates and 35–50 at Artificial Analysis rates.

Google acknowledged the problem. Antigravity's Varun Mohan said the cache-pool expansion "will increase what you can get done in quota by at least 20%". After resetting everyone's Gemini quota on 21 November, he put the gain at 22% ([X post](https://x.com/_mohansolo/status/2123848516581892181); [X post](https://x.com/_mohansolo/status/2124070033534161407)). One user disputes that: after the patch they measured only 57% cache hits on `gemini-4-neon-xhigh` ([t/203507](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/unacceptable-post-patch-reality-promised-92-96-cache-hit-rate-vs-57-actual-kv-cache-18s-server-evictions-unfair-sharding-on-new-paid-accounts/203507)).

Argon and Carbon do worse on caching. Users report **0% cache hits on Carbon and Argon** at 120K+ prefixes, against about 98% on Neon in the same workflow ([t/203048](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/ultra-feedback-telemetry-0-cache-hit-rate-on-carbon-argon-120k-tokens-21s-interval-vs-98-on-neon-unacceptable-quota-drain-for-long-context-workflows/203048)). One Carbon High turn collapsed mid-way and cost about 5.5× its counterfactual ([t/202768](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/telemetry-bug-report-why-antigravity-weekly-quotas-burn-so-fast-gemini-4-carbon-high-mid-turn-cache-collapse-5-5x-cost-5-min-implicit-cache-cliff/202768)). These problems are intermittent, but when they hit, Carbon falls further behind Neon than the 3× rate alone suggests.

Taken together, the practical guidance is to run Medium or High, keep contexts under about 200K, and avoid long idle gaps between calls.

## 1 December changes Argon and Carbon's dollar multiple, not Neon's

Launch pricing for Carbon and Argon ends on 30 November. From 1 December their API price doubles to **$4/$20/$0.20**. Neon stays at $3/$15/$0.15, because it never had a launch discount. Gemini 3.8 Flash doubles on 1 January 2027 ([Gemini API pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)).

Google has published nothing about how Antigravity quota will respond. A forum reply from an account whose role is not shown said promotional rates "apply only to pay-as-you-go API billing" and that fixed per-model multipliers "are not published" ([t/196351](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-4-argon-draws-antigravity-quota-at-3x-gemini-3-8-flash-per-published-dollar-clean-a-b-same-bucket/196351)). The plans page announces no December change ([Antigravity plans](https://antigravity.google/docs/plans)).

If nothing changes for Neon, Neon stays at **26.1×**. For Argon and Carbon, three outcomes are possible. In **Scenario A, quota keeps charging the current $6/$30 per million tokens**, which is 1.5× the new list price. The tokens a week buys are unchanged, but their list value doubles to $400 a week, or **17.4×** on Ultra 5x (about 34.8× on Ultra 20x by extrapolation). In **Scenario B, quota keeps 3× and applies it to the new price ($12/$60)**. The dollar multiple stays at **8.7×**, but each Argon or Carbon task costs twice as much quota: weekly Carbon High capacity halves to about 26 tasks, and Neon's work advantage at equal intelligence grows to about 6×. In **Scenario C, Argon and Carbon move to the blog's literal 1× rule** and reach 26.1×, matching Neon. Current behaviour contradicts this, and nothing suggests it is coming.

Scenario A has a suggestive coincidence behind it. The measured $6/$30 is exactly 1.5× the standard $4/$20, which fits the idea that quota already prices these models off an internal post-promotion rate. That remains speculation.

For a value comparison, the important point is that in Scenario A the multiple rises while the work stays the same. This project currently prices Argon and Carbon tasks at half of Artificial Analysis's standard rate and applies 8.7×. Under Scenario A it would price them at the full rate and apply 17.4×, and tasks per $20 would not move (Carbon High stays at about 44). Only Scenario B changes Argon and Carbon's real position, by halving it.

Either way, **Neon's lead in work per quota dollar survives 1 December at 3× or more**. The cleanest way to find out which scenario Google chose is to rerun the single-turn `/quota` A/B on 1 December against the new $4/$20 prices.

Two smaller points also matter after the deadline. AI credits, which pay for usage beyond the subscription quota, already bill Carbon at its 1× list price ([t/196351](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-4-argon-draws-antigravity-quota-at-3x-gemini-3-8-flash-per-published-dollar-clean-a-b-same-bucket/196351)). For heavy Argon or Carbon users, a credit dollar already buys three times what a quota dollar does. And the 1 January Flash doubling raises a separate question: is the pool fixed in dollars or in Flash tokens? If it is fixed in Flash tokens, every cross-model ratio, Neon's included, would be rescaled a month later.

## Conclusion

At 26.1× on Ultra 5x and 22.8× on Pro, an Antigravity subscription used on Neon is worth more than twenty times its fee. That works out to roughly 3–6× more equal-quality work per week than the same quota spent on Carbon. The earlier, more cautious transferred figure of 8.7× understated Neon by exactly the 3× penalty Google applies to Argon and Carbon. The quota rate is no longer the uncertain part of this valuation. What remains uncertain is how well caching works on a given day, the unmeasured Ultra 20x pool, and Google's still-unannounced 1 December rules.

These figures rest on one account per tier, measured on a single day, and Google explicitly reserves the right to resize pools with capacity. They are a snapshot, not a contract. The most useful follow-ups are a post-1 December A/B for Argon and Carbon and a receipted Ultra 20x week. Until those exist, 26.1× is the best-supported Neon figure for Ultra 5x, and any Ultra 20x number is an extrapolation.
