Method, assumptions & sources

A common spending denominator

Tasks / subscription $ = multiplier ÷ AA cost / task
Tasks / $20 = 20 × rounded tasks / subscription $

All task counts are estimates. Tasks per subscription dollar are rounded to one decimal before multiplying by 20. The subscription-spend scenario scales these estimates by subscription fees / $20. API-equivalent values are calculation inputs; the budget itself is the amount paid for the subscription.

The default intelligence floor is 46. Lower it to include Luna, Haiku, and other lower-scoring reasoning configurations. Fable is excluded because it is not available on all plans. Only configurations with published benchmark values are included.

Reliable bug-fix capability

Bug weight = lowest intelligence of a 100% solver
Bug-fix rating = sum of weights of reliable fixes
Subscription $ / bug fixed
= source run cost ÷ multiplier ÷ (planted fixes + extra fixes)

A reliable fix means a bug was verified fixed in every recorded run of the exact model and effort level, with at least two runs and complete per-bug coverage required. Astra is the sole exception to the two-run minimum: its single-run verified fixes are included in ratings and difficulty weights, without evidence of repeatability. Other single runs and partial coverage are excluded. Each bug’s weight is the lowest Artificial Analysis intelligence score among reliable solvers in the 33 configurations included here. Bugs without a reliable solver remain unrated. Weights use the full dataset and stay fixed when explorer filters change.

The bug-fix rating sums the difficulty weights of reliable fixes, crediting both fix volume and inferred difficulty. Configurations with no reliable fixes have no rating and are excluded. It is an empirical difficulty proxy, not an official benchmark score or a calibrated measure of difficulty. Estimated subscription cost per bug fixed is the source run cost divided by our subscription multiplier and total fixes: planted fixes plus unplanted extras. We use published mean fix counts and mean costs for repeated runs, or the single run for the Astra exception. Partial fixes and claimed-only bugs are excluded. Extras affect cost only; they receive no intelligence weight because they lack a withheld answer key. Their coverage cannot be verified across runs from these aggregate counts. Rating and unit cost do not change with subscription spend. A 100% observed rate over a few runs does not guarantee future reliability.

The Bug Hunt snapshot is dated 2026-10-08. We match exact model and effort levels, omit superseded rows, and use the published current means. Only verified planted fixes contribute to the horizontal rating; unplanted extras also contribute to the vertical cost denominator. We assume first-party harnesses and make no adjustment for Grok Build versus Cursor. Grok costs are reconstructed lower bounds, so its subscription costs per bug fixed are lower bounds. Other matched costs are list-rate estimates. Artificial Analysis supplies the intelligence scores used to derive bug weights. Bugs mode filters by bug-fix rating; all bug-fix ratings are included by default. Tasks mode retains the intelligence floor of 46.

What counts as Pareto optimal?

In tasks mode, no other included configuration offers at least as much intelligence and throughput. In bugs mode, no other offers at least as high a bug-fix rating and at most as much subscription cost per bug fixed. Dominance requires a strict improvement in at least one dimension. The explorer recomputes the frontier among the configurations matching your search, subscription, and floor filters.

A snapshot, not a usage guarantee

The Claude and ChatGPT multipliers are estimates derived from SemiAnalysis's API-equivalent subscription-value analysis. The same provider multipliers are applied across all included models and reasoning levels: Claude 58.9× and ChatGPT 10.6×. This is a comparison assumption, not a separate measurement for each model. Cursor's multiplier is calculated from the author's usage exports: metered API-equivalent usage divided by subscription spend.

The Cursor exports included both Grok and Claude usage. Applying the full 20.7× multiplier to Grok assumes both available usage pools can be allocated to it. Benchmark tasks are a spending proxy; actual task volume depends on the workload and usage limits. Figures reflect the 8 October 2026 snapshot.
