Method, assumptions & sources

A common spending denominator

Tasks / subscription $ = multiplier ÷ AA cost / task
Tasks / $20 = 20 × rounded tasks / subscription $

All task counts are estimates. Tasks per subscription dollar are rounded to one decimal before multiplying by 20. The subscription-spend scenario scales these estimates by subscription fees / $20. API-equivalent values are calculation inputs. The budget represents subscription fees, with Fable normalized to dollars allocated to its half-plan share (full-plan equivalent).

The default intelligence floor is 46. Lower it to include Luna, Haiku, and other lower-scoring reasoning configurations. Astra uses 14.5× from SemiAnalysis’s $200/month ChatGPT Pro example. Fable uses 24.8×: the study’s rounded 12.4× divided by its 50% plan allocation, normalizing value per allocated subscription dollar. This does not increase Fable’s actual allowance or include the remaining half-plan’s value. These model-specific assumptions, updated 23 October 2026 from the 5 October study, apply across reasoning levels. Only configurations with published benchmark values are included.

Reliable bug-fix capability

Bug weight = lowest intelligence of a 100% solver
Difficulty credit = sum of weights of reliable fixes
Miss penalty per bug = penalty strength × lowest bug weight² ÷ bug weight
Bug-fix rating = difficulty credit − total miss penalty
Subscription $ / bug fixed
= source run cost × price factor ÷ multiplier ÷ (planted fixes + extra fixes)

A reliable fix means a bug was verified fixed in every recorded run of the exact model and effort level, with at least one run and complete per-bug coverage required. Single-run verified fixes are included for every model and can set difficulty weights, but they are labeled because repeatability is unverified. Partial coverage is excluded. Each bug’s weight is the lowest Artificial Analysis intelligence score among reliable solvers that have one. Grok 4.7 Medium and Muse Low, Medium, and High have no published score: they can earn weights in bugs mode but do not set them, and they appear only in that mode. Bugs without a reliable solver remain unrated. Weights use the full dataset and stay fixed when explorer filters change.

The bug-fix rating subtracts a penalty for every rated planted bug not fixed in every run. Each miss costs the penalty strength times the square of the lowest calibrated bug weight divided by that bug’s weight. Use 0 to turn deductions off, 2 for the default, or a higher value for stronger penalties. At 1×, the easiest miss costs as much as its fix credit; harder misses cost progressively less, without an intelligence cutoff. The same weights apply to every configuration, including those without an AA score. At strengths up to 1×, harder fixes still earn more than easier fixes. Higher strengths increasingly favor coverage of easier bugs. Every additional fix improves the rating. Negative scores mean miss penalties exceed fix credit; they remain visible under No floor. Unrated bugs receive neither credit nor penalty. Configurations with no calibrated reliable fixes have no rating and are excluded. The minimum weight and all miss penalties can change when the dataset changes. It is an empirical difficulty proxy, not an official benchmark score or a calibrated measure of difficulty. Estimated subscription cost per bug fixed is the source run cost, adjusted for any recorded token-price conversion, divided by our subscription multiplier and total fixes: planted fixes plus unplanted extras. We use published mean fix counts and mean costs for repeated runs, or the single run where only one complete run is available. Partial fixes and claimed-only bugs are excluded. Extras affect cost only; they receive no intelligence weight because they lack a withheld answer key. Their coverage cannot be verified across runs from these aggregate counts. Rating and unit cost do not change with subscription spend. A 100% observed rate over a few runs does not guarantee future reliability.

What counts as Pareto optimal?

In tasks mode, no other included configuration offers at least as much intelligence and throughput. In bugs mode, no other offers at least as high a bug-fix rating and at most as much subscription cost per bug fixed. Dominance requires a strict improvement in at least one dimension. The explorer normally recomputes the frontier among the configurations matching your search and floor filters. Add pareto to the search to include the full-dataset frontier and its line, ignoring other search terms for the frontier points while still applying the selected floor to displayed points and line; china,pareto shows matching Chinese models plus that unfiltered frontier. The active metric and early-reset scenario still apply.

A snapshot, not a usage guarantee

The Claude and ChatGPT multipliers are estimates derived from SemiAnalysis's API-equivalent subscription-value analysis. Default multipliers are Claude 58.9× and ChatGPT 10.6×, with model-specific exceptions of Fable 24.8× and Astra 14.5×. Each multiplier applies across that model’s reasoning levels as a comparison assumption. Fable is normalized per allocated subscription dollar; its 50% cap still limits actual use. The source study is dated 5 October 2026, not a live quota feed. Cursor's multiplier is calculated from the author's usage exports: metered API-equivalent usage divided by subscription spend.

The Cursor exports included both Grok and Claude usage. Applying the full 20.7× multiplier to Grok assumes both available usage pools can be allocated to it. Benchmark tasks are a spending proxy; actual task volume depends on the workload and usage limits. Original figures reflect the 8 October 2026 snapshot; Fable was added on 10 October and Muse on 12 October 2026.
