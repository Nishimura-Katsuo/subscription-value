Method, assumptions & sources

Current intelligence snapshot

Artificial Analysis v5.0 inputs checked 27 October 2026: 53 configurations with both General Intelligence and Engineering Intelligence scores; 50 also have matching Bug Hunt results. Configurations without published v5.0 scores are excluded from both modes. Scores are rounded to whole numbers. General Intelligence methodology · Engineering Index. Engineering combines seven evaluations across broad engineering work; it is not solely a software engineering score.

The selector changes task capability, bug difficulty calibration, ratings, floors, sorting, and Pareto frontiers. Task throughput and task costs continue to use the General Intelligence benchmark workload; bug costs use Bug Hunt run evidence. Each metric and basis remembers its own floor. The default task floors are General ≥38 and Engineering ≥43. Bugs default to No floor.

A common spending denominator

Tasks / subscription $ = multiplier ÷ matched benchmark cost / task
Tasks / $20 = 20 × rounded tasks / subscription $

Task cost is rounded to cents, or four decimals for sub-cent values; tasks per dollar are rounded to one decimal before multiplying by 20. Spend scenarios scale these estimates linearly and represent subscription fees only, excluding API credits and on-demand charges. CSV preserves exact AA inputs and the task-price conversion factor. MiMo and Qwen task costs are repriced to OpenCode Go using AA’s weighted token-cost breakdown under the same-cache assumption; the displayed benchmark cost already includes that conversion. DeepSeek task inputs use peak prices and Bug Hunt bills use off-peak prices, each matched to Go rates.

Reliable bug-fix capability

Bug weight = lowest selected-index score of a reliable solver
Difficulty credit = sum of reliable fixed-bug weights
Miss penalty = strength × sum(lowest weight² ÷ missed-bug weight)
Bug-fix rating = difficulty credit − miss penalty
Subscription $ / bug fixed = source cost × price factor
÷ multiplier ÷ (planted fixes + extra fixes)

A reliable fix is verified in every recorded run of an exact model and effort, with complete per-bug coverage. Single runs count and can calibrate weights, but are labeled because repeatability is unverified. Both bases are calibrated once from the full dataset; search, floors, and penalty strength do not recalibrate them. Bugs without a reliable solver receive neither credit nor penalty. Configurations without calibrated reliable fixes are unrated. Extras affect cost only; their coverage cannot be verified across runs from aggregate counts.

Penalty strength defaults to 2; 0 disables it. At 1×, the easiest miss costs its fix credit; harder misses cost less without an intelligence cutoff. Higher strengths increasingly favor coverage of easier bugs. Negative ratings remain included under No floor. This empirical difficulty proxy is not an official benchmark score. Repeated runs use published mean costs and fixes; partial and claimed-only fixes are excluded. Observed reliability does not guarantee future performance. Rating and unit cost do not change with spend.

What counts as Pareto optimal?

Task dominance means at least as high a selected intelligence score and throughput; bug dominance means at least as high a rating and at most as much cost per fix. One dimension must improve strictly. The frontier normally uses matches to search and floor. Add pareto to include the full-dataset frontier alongside search matches, still applying the floor to displayed points and line. The active basis and early-reset scenario apply throughout.
